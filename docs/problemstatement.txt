PROJECT: AI-Powered Discovery Engine for Myntra Wishlist-to-Purchase Conversion
PART: 1 — Build an AI-Powered Discovery Engine

ROLE AND CONTEXT
You are a Product Manager on the Growth Team at Myntra.

Myntra has millions of users who browse fashion products, save items they like, and add products to their wishlists. A wishlist is an important high-intent signal because the user has explicitly expressed interest in a product but has not yet purchased it.

The business objective is to increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.

However, the underlying user problem is NOT provided. The purpose of Part 1 is to discover that problem before proposing a solution.

PART 1 OBJECTIVE
Build an AI-powered Discovery Engine that analyzes public user conversations at scale to identify, quantify where possible, compare, and prioritize potential user problems and opportunity areas that may influence wishlist-to-purchase conversion.

The Discovery Engine must go beyond simple review summarization or sentiment analysis. It should convert unstructured user conversations into structured, evidence-backed insights that can support product decision-making.

RESEARCH SOURCE CONSTRAINT
For this project, the Discovery Engine must use ONLY the following four source types:

1. Apple App Store reviews
2. Google Play Store reviews
3. Reddit discussions/comments
4. YouTube comments

Do not use other external discovery sources for the engine unless this constraint is explicitly changed later.

CORE DISCOVERY QUESTIONS
The engine should help investigate the following questions:

1. Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist?
2. What prevents wishlisted products from eventually being purchased?
3. What uncertainties remain after a user has identified a product they like?
4. What causes users to postpone a purchase?
5. How do users compare multiple shortlisted or wishlisted products?
6. What information do users seek outside Myntra before purchasing?
7. What role do fit and size, styling, price, reviews, occasion, and social validation play in purchase decisions?
8. When is the wishlist used as genuine purchase intent versus simple bookmarking or saving for later?
9. How do these behaviors differ across meaningful user segments?
10. What unmet needs appear repeatedly across user conversations?
11. Which opportunity areas appear most likely to influence wishlist-to-purchase conversion?

WHAT THE ENGINE SHOULD DO
The system should create a repeatable workflow that can:

A. Collect or ingest user conversations from the four allowed sources.

B. Clean and normalize the collected text while preserving the original evidence and source context.

C. Identify relevant conversations related to fashion shopping, wishlisting/saving, purchase hesitation, purchase decision-making, and post-save behavior.

D. Extract structured signals from each relevant conversation, such as:
   - User intent
   - Reason for saving/wishlisting
   - Purchase intent
   - Purchase blocker
   - Uncertainty
   - Information need
   - Consideration of alternatives
   - Current workaround
   - Trigger for purchase
   - Trigger for postponement
   - Context/occasion
   - Product/category context where available

E. Cluster recurring problems and unmet needs into coherent opportunity themes.

F. Distinguish between different use cases of the wishlist, especially:
   - High-intent purchase consideration
   - Comparison/shortlisting
   - Price or timing monitoring
   - Bookmarking/inspiration
   - Temporary saving with no clear purchase intent

G. Segment insights where the available evidence supports meaningful differences, for example by shopping context, user need, product category, or behavioral intent. Do not create unsupported demographic segments.

H. Quantify opportunity areas where possible. Quantification should include the denominator, source volume, and calculation logic so that percentages are interpretable and auditable.

I. Compare opportunity areas using a consistent prioritization method based on evidence and potential relevance to the business metric.

J. Preserve traceability from each major insight back to the underlying source evidence. Every important insight should be supported by examples or source references.

K. Produce a structured output that can be used by a Product Manager to decide which opportunity should be validated through primary user research.

RECOMMENDED ANALYSIS PIPELINE
The engine should follow a pipeline similar to:

Source Collection
→ Data Cleaning
→ Relevance Filtering
→ User Intent/Behavior Extraction
→ Problem & Pain-Point Extraction
→ Thematic Clustering
→ User/Need Segmentation
→ Evidence Quantification
→ Opportunity Comparison/Prioritization
→ Evidence-Backed Discovery Report

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS
The engine must not assume that every wishlist item represents strong purchase intent.

The engine must distinguish:
- "User likes the product" from "User intends to purchase the product"
- "User is postponing purchase" from "User has no purchase intent"
- "User needs more information" from "User simply wants a bookmark"
- "Price is mentioned" from "Price uncertainty is actually preventing purchase"

The system must avoid treating sentiment alone as the problem. A negative review does not automatically represent a wishlist-to-purchase problem.

The engine should focus on behavioral barriers, unmet needs, uncertainties, decision friction, and workarounds that are plausibly connected to the journey from wishlist/save to purchase.

OPPORTUNITY AREAS TO INVESTIGATE
Do not assume these are the final problems. Treat them as hypotheses to investigate through evidence:

- Fit and size confidence
- Product quality confidence
- Price uncertainty or waiting for a better price
- Uncertainty about whether the product is worth buying
- Difficulty comparing shortlisted products
- Lack of styling or outfit context
- Occasion-based uncertainty
- Insufficient reviews or social proof
- Lack of trustworthy user-generated information
- Availability/size/color uncertainty
- Waiting for the right moment to purchase
- Wishlist overload and difficulty revisiting saved products
- Saving products primarily for bookmarking rather than purchase
- Need for information outside the app before purchase
- Other recurring barriers discovered from user conversations

EXPECTED OUTPUT OF THE DISCOVERY ENGINE
The engine should produce a structured discovery output containing at minimum:

1. Executive summary of the strongest findings.
2. Top recurring user problems/opportunity themes.
3. Evidence volume for each theme.
4. Relative prevalence or estimated share where measurable.
5. Representative source evidence for each important theme.
6. User intent/behavior associated with each theme.
7. Existing user workaround, when observable.
8. Relevant user segment or context, when supported by evidence.
9. Estimated relationship to wishlist-to-purchase conversion, clearly labeled as evidence, inference, or hypothesis.
10. Opportunity prioritization/comparison.
11. Recommended opportunity area(s) for primary user research.
12. Clear list of unresolved questions that interviews need to validate.

EVIDENCE AND TRUST REQUIREMENTS
Every major conclusion must be traceable to collected evidence.

The system should:
- Keep source attribution.
- Store source text or a suitable excerpt/reference where permitted.
- Separate observed evidence from AI-generated interpretation.
- Label assumptions and hypotheses clearly.
- Avoid fabricating user feedback.
- Avoid presenting unsupported percentages as facts.
- Avoid overgeneralizing from a small or biased sample.

MVP EXPECTATION FOR PART 1
Build the simplest functional version of the Discovery Engine that demonstrates the full flow from the four allowed sources to structured, prioritized opportunity insights.

The goal of Part 1 is NOT to design the final Myntra feature.
The goal is to build a reliable discovery system that helps answer:

"What user problem is most likely preventing wishlist items from becoming purchases within 30 days, and what evidence supports that conclusion?"

OUT OF SCOPE FOR PART 1
Do not:
- Design the final product solution before discovery.
- Assume a specific root cause without evidence.
- Introduce monetary incentives.
- Rely only on sentiment analysis.
- Treat review ratings as a substitute for behavioral research.
- Use sources outside App Store, Play Store, Reddit, and YouTube comments.
- Treat AI-generated hypotheses as validated user truths.

SUCCESS CRITERIA FOR THE DISCOVERY ENGINE
The Part 1 system should be considered successful when it can:

1. Process relevant user conversations from the four permitted source types.
2. Identify recurring purchase barriers and unmet needs.
3. Distinguish different wishlist intents and behaviors.
4. Quantify or estimate prevalence where the data supports it.
5. Compare and prioritize opportunity areas using a transparent method.
6. Show source-level evidence for key conclusions.
7. Produce a shortlist of high-potential problems that can be taken into 5–6 user interviews for validation.

FINAL DECISION OUTPUT
At the end of Part 1, the Discovery Engine should help the Product Manager move from:

Raw user conversations
→ Evidence
→ Behavioral patterns
→ Problem themes
→ Opportunity areas
→ Prioritized hypotheses
→ Interview questions for validation

The selected problem must ultimately be determined through the combination of AI discovery evidence and primary user research, not by assumption.
